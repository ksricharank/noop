# 11.6.0.17.0 (build 372)

A **pure uplift**: the 16.24 feature set, rebased onto upstream **v11.6.0**. No new
functionality. New features resume at 17.1.

Upstream shipped 11.5.0 and 11.6.0 since the v11.1.0 pin — **271 commits, 715 files,
+74,281 lines** — and unlike the 38-commit v16 uplift, this one landed squarely in the
fork's own territory.

## What upstream built in our territory

| Upstream | Ours | Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| Coach: streaming, persisted history, voice, morning brief, Siri (#1862) | LLM plumbing: fallback, retry, timeout budget, horizons | **Complementary.** Ours rebased onto their streaming. |
| HR widget (#1957), stress widget (#2044/#2045) | Targets widget, Live Activity, Dynamic Island | Different widgets; shared plumbing merged. |
| Steps-calibration persistence (#2023): 32.9s → 3.3s | Locked re-score deferral | **Compose.** Theirs makes a pass cheaper, ours makes fewer passes run. |
| Sleep-aware physiological day cycles (#1572) | Day-quality score | See the open question below. |
| WHOOP 5 R-R units (#2046), skin temp in pass 2 (#2042) | — | Zero fork contact. |

**Nothing of ours was redundant.** Two findings worth re-checking at the next uplift, because
both contradict what the release notes imply:

- **Per-provider API-key slots are still needed.** v11.6.0 still stores one shared
  `account = "api-key"` with a UserDefaults owner marker. Upstream's #2058 repairs a
  *rejected* key; it does not give each provider its own slot.
- **The bond-loop re-park is still needed.** `bondLoopParkCycles` is absent at v11.6.0, and
  `standingConnectWhilePausedIfDue` is unchanged at its five call sites. Upstream's BLE work
  in this range was diagnostics in other functions.

## How the conflicts went

Every conflict was **complementary**, not competing — the resolutions keep both sides. The
ones that needed a real decision:

- **`saveAndReloadIfChanged`** became `-> Bool` on our side while upstream added branches to
  it. Every branch now returns.
- **Notification taps** now have three handlers (coach brief, water undo, hydration add).
  Undo runs before the add actions; the coach route is non-consuming.
- **Light-pass recovery.** Upstream's skin-temp/day-cycle enrichment still runs; only the
  baseline-relative recompute is gated.
- **Android `httpError`.** A rejected key is *not* retryable — a fallback model would be
  turned away by the same key. Verified this matches Swift's existing
  `deservesLighterModelRetry`, which already excludes `.badKey`.
- **Tab bar.** Upstream promoted Coach to a tab; we insert Day quality. The fork's order
  wins (Coach stays reachable from More and from routing).

## What the compiler and tests caught that review did not

Six defects, every one from a "keep both sides" splice that was wrong in a way reading the
diff did not reveal:

1. **Three lost closing braces** (`estimatedTokens`, `saveRepairedKey`, the `morningBriefBar`
   card whose tail had been replaced by our `.task`/`.sheet` modifiers).
2. **Two duplicate declarations** — `lastTimeoutFallbackModel`, `openCoach()` — where the two
   "sides" were the *same* declaration.
3. **An unterminated `#if os(iOS)`** from grafting upstream's voice members.
4. **Eight init assignments stranded inside `localDayNumber`** — type-checked on macOS,
   failed only on the iOS leg.
5. **A static/instance mismatch** on `recomputeRecovery`.
6. **A near-regression caught in review, not by a tool:** our rate-limit commit predates
   upstream's streaming and used the old non-streaming `callProvider`. Taking our side
   verbatim would have silently deleted streaming replies. Same shape in CoachView, where our
   "move config to Settings" commit would have deleted upstream's voice input, follow-up
   chips, token estimate, Save-to-Journal and key-repair panel — all of which live only in
   that file.

This is what "app-target Swift has no CI" costs. A brace/`#if` balance check was added to the
resolution loop after the third instance.

## Open question for 17.1 — `dayCycleMode`

Upstream's #1572 lets steps, energy and Effort be owned by a **physiological day** (sleep
onset to sleep onset) instead of the calendar, and it **defaults to sleep-onset**.

The day-quality score reads exactly those fields, and its normal-day anchor (4,000 steps,
1,600 kcal, effort 8) was calibrated against **calendar** days. This is not a merge conflict —
it merges cleanly and the scores simply shift. Decide deliberately:

- **Calendar** preserves the calibrated anchor.
- **Sleep-onset** is upstream's default and arguably the better model, but the anchor
  constants should be re-checked against a few days of real data before trusting the numbers.
