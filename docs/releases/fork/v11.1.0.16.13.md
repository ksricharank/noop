## The widget lag: answered, and there is nothing to fix

The 320 instrumentation finally had a full day behind it (`window=00:03:20-09:09:23`):

    Widget publish today: full=107/107 live=5 reloads=28 dedup=79
    Widget background:    publishes=76 reloads=18 dedup=58 unseen=0
    Widget timelines:     requested=28 served=60

**`unseen=0`** is the answer. That counter is background publishes where the data changed and we did
NOT ask for a reload — zero across nine hours, so we never sat on a change. `dedup=58` of 76 is
correct suppression (identical on every rendered field). And `served=60` against `requested=28` means
iOS built MORE timelines than we asked for: our requests are not being dropped.

So the lag is WidgetKit choosing when to composite, not the app withholding data. 18 background
reloads against a ~40–70/day budget leaves headroom, but spending it would not help — the requests
already made are being honoured and then some. No change shipped; the measurement is the result.

## One tap, one cup

The report was "the water tap is too sensitive". The strap was innocent — it reported exactly ONE
`SENSORS: IMU double tap detected`, and the app logged two cups, at 21:28:13 and 21:28:14.

The cause is structural. During an offload the same EVENT reaches the gesture handlers through TWO
paths: `handle(parsed:)` and the mid-offload carve-out `dispatchLiveGestureIfFresh` (#69). The strap
also re-sends the event inside its 45 s freshness window. The 1.2 s wall-clock debounce in `AppModel`
could not cover that — the deliveries landed ~1.3 s apart, and the real gap is however long an
offload chunk takes, so no fixed window is safe.

The fix deduplicates on the event's OWN `event_timestamp` (the strap RTC's real-unix second, already
parsed and already used by the freshness gate) rather than on arrival time. That makes the duplicate
structurally impossible instead of merely unlikely: a timestamp IS the gesture's identity, whereas a
debounce guesses at identity from timing. Keyed per event name, cleared per connection, and it fails
OPEN when no timestamp is present — silently swallowing a real tap is the worse failure, since a
double-tap has no on-screen feedback by definition.

A triple-tap was considered and is not the answer: the firmware exposes a single double-tap gesture,
and the sensitivity was never the problem.

## The synthesis strip follows the day picker

Picking a previous date moved the rest of the synthesis section but left Steps, Cal, Effort and Water
on today's numbers. `DailyTargetsStrip` took no day at all and read `repo.cachedLiveTargets()`, which
is anchored to today three separate ways — `now: Date()` → `todayKey`, a `cachedWidgetAnchor(now:)`
behind every denominator, and the today-only hydration cache — and whose memo key does not include
the browsed day, so even a corrected caller would have been handed today's bundle straight back.

`Repository.liveTargets(forDay:)` is the day-scoped read, mirroring the existing
`waterCupsAndTarget(forDay:)`: filter `days` to that day and earlier so the baselines see only what
was known THEN, and take the charge band from that day's own row.

**`cachedLiveTargets()` is deliberately untouched.** It also feeds the Live Activity, the widget
faces, the pacing notifications and the coach, and every one of those must stay on today — a widget
quoting a browsed historical day would be a worse bug than the stale strip. A test pins that the
widget and coach paths never take the day-scoped road.

A browsed day with no row renders empty rather than falling back to the live bundle; the fallback
would print today's numbers under a past date, which is the original defect wearing a new hat.

The coach paragraph had the same leak and is now gated on offset 0 in BOTH Today implementations —
`synthesisIsCurrent` is a freshness check, not a day match, so it was narrating today under older
dates.

## Day quality gets its own tab

A fifth tab between Trends and Sleep, per the maintainer's placement. The screen is Sleep's
per-day browsing above and Trends' rollups below: chevrons over the SCORED days (unscored days are
skipped, so stepping back always lands on a real score), the existing `DayQualityCard` for the score,
breakdown and narrative, a windowed trend chart with its own 14/30/90 selector, and a week-in-review
band tally.

The card itself gained only a `dayIndex` — the compact card still on Trends and the detail screen are
one implementation, not two, so they cannot drift. Its "Yesterday" overline now names the actual day
(`Yesterday` / `Today` / weekday), because a hard-coded "Yesterday" over a day three weeks back is
the same class of lie as the stale strip above.

Inserting a tab renumbers every tag after it. The More tab moved 3 → 4 (its coach deep-link with it),
and the swipe clamp `min(3, …)` — which would have made the last tab unreachable by swipe while every
other route still worked — is now `min(Self.lastTabIndex, …)` so the bound and the tab list cannot
drift apart again.

## Re-score: measurement, not a fix

The log showed NINE full 21-night passes in one day, two of them ~3 minutes (`175072 ms`,
`168947 ms`), 25 light passes, and 41 "already outstanding" deferrals, against MetricKit's
`bg=151m`. That makes re-scoring the leading candidate for the day's background CPU — but the
per-pass lines cannot say which trigger keeps starting work that is then thrown away, and the 41
deferrals name no trigger at all.

`RescoreStats` tallies the whole day per trigger: started, completed, deferred, and the milliseconds
actually burned, split foreground/background (the two call for opposite fixes, so they are never
summed). Deferrals bucket by CAUSE rather than trigger — the scheduler that makes the call does not
know which trigger woke it, and "outstanding ×41" vs "locked ×41" is the actionable distinction
anyway. `Decision.deferToBackgroundTask` now carries a stable token beside its reason string, so a
reworded reason cannot silently split a counter in two.

No scheduling behaviour changed. Instrumentation-first: cheap counters on paths already logging, and
the next log picks the fix.
