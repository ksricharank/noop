## What the 31 archived logs establish (no new wiring needed)

| Era | per-statement SQL | longest pass |
|---|---|---|
| 260906–260919 (11.1 → 11.6) | 1–880 ms | 33–401 s (one 4214 s foreground outlier, the #1538 era) |

| 260926–28 | multi-second routine, worst **24 s/stmt** | **2272 s light** |

iOS constant (27.0) across the whole window. So: **the degradation begins with the v11.8.0
uplift week (260920–22), not with this week's builds.** Pass stretching arrives first (18.15 —
the same stack that reconciled upstream #2296's CPU pacing into the loop), store-level slowness
(seconds per SQL statement) by 260922, worsening daily since.

The Health-app battery share is DOWNSTREAM of the same slowdown: today's 912 health wakes are not
unprecedented (764 on 260827, build 10.6.0.4), but `avgSyncMs` went from ~2–13 s to 175–254 s at
exactly the same inflection — each unit of health work costs 10–100× more. The 30-min write-back
floor (18.28) stays as a volume cap; it is not claimed as the cause.

## What the logs cannot decide — and what this build measures

Why a statement takes seconds: a checkpoint-starved WAL every read must walk (favoured: the
long-lived process + 1 Hz live writer + always-on readers, and `checkpointWAL()` only ever runs
after bulk imports), background I/O throttling, or store-actor contention. Two measurements,
nothing else changed:

- **Header**: `Store files: data=XMB wal=YMB`.
- **Every pass's sql line** gains ` paced=Xms wal=YMB` — the pass's deliberate pacing sleep split
  out of its wall (pacing rest is proportional to measured wall INCLUDING store waits, so a
  contended store also earns longer sleeps; the tally shows whether that feedback compounds).

Verdict rule for the next log: `wal=` large (hundreds of MB) and climbing alongside the slow
passes ⇒ WAL starvation confirmed, fix is periodic checkpointing. Small and flat ⇒ theory dead,
the I/O-throttle/contention fork is next. No fix ships until the numbers pick one.
