# 11.1.0.16.14 (build 326)

Three maintainer reports: a step target that moved in 2,000-step jumps, a day-quality score graded
against the wrong night, and a widget-lag answer that was stated too strongly.

## The step target is continuous in charge

Reported as: "can we make the steps target more granular vs just 6000, 8000, and 10k? something
almost continuous?"

It was three hard bands. Charge 34 and charge 66 both asked for exactly 8,000, and charge 66→67
jumped the ask by 2,000 steps on a single point of charge. The bands were never a physiological
claim — the population evidence supports the 7–10k RANGE, not three points inside it — so the cliff
was an artefact of the encoding.

The charge term now interpolates, rounded to 50 steps. Charge 40 asks 6,800; charge 55 asks 8,600.
Across charge 0–100 the target takes **35 distinct values instead of 3**, and no single point of
charge moves it by more than 200.

**The three published anchors are preserved exactly** — recover ceiling → 6k, midpoint → 8k, push
floor → 10k — so this refines the curve BETWEEN the published points rather than moving them. Any
day that previously sat on a band edge keeps its number, and the existing ladder test still pins the
same values it always did.

Piecewise-linear through the anchors rather than one straight line: the two halves have different
slopes (2k over 17 points of charge vs 2k over 34), and flattening them would move the maintain
figure off 8k for the mid-charge days that are the most common case. Outside the anchors the value
is held, not extrapolated — charge 5 and charge 33 are both "recover", and the readiness notch is
what handles a genuinely rundown body. The notch and the 4k–12k clamp are unchanged.

Note this moves the denominator of the steps n/t, so re-scored day-quality values shift slightly.

## Day quality: the night that CONCLUDES the day

Reported as: "the day quality score seems to use yesterday night's sleep instead of tonight's sleep
(which I count as part of yesterday — i.e., the sleep is the conclusion of the day)".

Correct, and the mismatch was real. A sleep session is attributed to the day its END falls on
(`AnalyticsEngine.analyzeDay`), so row D holds the night D-1→D — the night that PRECEDED day D's
waking hours. The scorer read `row.totalSleepMin` off the same row, so it graded day D's steps,
calories and effort against the sleep that came BEFORE the work.

The recovery half now reads row D+1: the night that closed the day.

**All three recovery signals move together.** HRV and resting HR are measured during that same
night, so leaving them behind would make the half incoherent — the sleep after the day against the
autonomic response to the day before it. The EXECUTION half stays exactly where it was; that is the
day's own work and was never wrong.

The baseline still excludes the night being graded (`< day` now excludes both the day and its
concluding night), so a night cannot be its own yardstick.

**A day is scorable only once its concluding night has landed.** In practice the newest score is the
day before yesterday rather than yesterday. That is the accepted cost of the change (maintainer's
call: publish complete, not early) and it keeps the scorer's existing rule 3 — nothing published
until it is complete — rather than emitting a recovery-less score at breakfast and revising it. A
day whose concluding night never arrives stays unscored rather than being graded on execution alone,
since a score missing half its inputs is not comparable with the series around it.

Existing stored scores were computed under the old attribution. They re-score on the next config
change; the series will shift when they do.

## The widget budget: the claim was too strong

The 16.13 notes said the lag "is not ours and there is nothing to fix". The first half is supported;
the second was not, and the maintainer pushed back on exactly the right point — 18 background
reloads against a ~40–70/day budget IS headroom.

What the 260906 log actually proved: `unseen=0` (we never sat on a change) and `served=60` against
`requested=28` (iOS built MORE timelines than we asked for). That establishes the app is not
withholding requests. It does NOT establish that the served timelines reach the screen promptly —
`served` counts builds, not repaints — so "nothing can be improved" did not follow.

That leaves two places the delay can live, and no counter could tell them apart:

1. between our reload request and `getTimeline` running (WidgetKit scheduling), or
2. between `getTimeline` returning and the system compositing (outside the app entirely).

The app stamps each reload request into the App Group; the extension measures against it when
`getTimeline` runs. The log line gains `reqToBuild=Ns avg / Ns max over N`.

Only measured when a request is actually outstanding — WidgetKit also refreshes on its own `.after`
schedule, and timing those against a stale stamp would invent a lag nobody waited on. The stamp is
consumed, so each request counts once; implausible values (a clock move, a stamp that outlived its
request) are discarded rather than banked. Absent until something is measured: a "0s avg" printed
before any reload has been served would claim WidgetKit is instant, which the counter has not
earned.

Attribution before spending, per the maintainer's call. A large mean points at scheduling, where
app-side levers might help; a small mean with a visibly stale face points at compositing, which
would end the search honestly rather than inviting another speculative fix.
