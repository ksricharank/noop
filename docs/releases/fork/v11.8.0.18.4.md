# 11.8.0.18.4 (build 405)

One fix: the choppy scrolling introduced by 18.0, on every tab.

## What happened

Adding the per-tab LLM summaries put `@EnvironmentObject var coach: AICoachEngine` on three heavy
tab roots — `TrendsView`, `SleepView` and `DayQualityView`.

`@EnvironmentObject` subscribes to the **whole object's** `objectWillChange`, regardless of which
properties the body actually reads. `AICoachEngine` has **32** `@Published` properties. One of
them, `synthesisRefreshing`, toggles while a summary generates — which, now that all three
summaries open by default, is precisely when the wearer lands on the tab and starts scrolling.
Every publish re-evaluated a ~2000-line body in full.

Two things make this worse than a simple oversight:

- **`SleepView` documents this exact hazard**, a few lines above where the declaration went. It
  deliberately does not observe `LiveState` or `AppModel`, because a ~1 Hz publish would
  re-evaluate its 3000-line body, and its live dependencies are scoped to small leaves instead. A
  32-property object went in directly underneath that comment.
- **None of the three bodies read the engine at all.** They captured it for the `generate` closure
  and nothing else. The subscription bought nothing and cost everything.

## The fix

`TabInsightCard` — the leaf that actually uses it — now owns the engine, and `generate` takes it as
a parameter. The router got the same treatment: it publishes far less often and is not the
scrolling cost, but those three observations were also new, also unread, and the button that needs
it lives in the card.

`TrendsView` and `SleepView` now observe exactly what they observed at v11.8.0; `DayQualityView`
exactly what it observed before this feature batch.

## Why the test is source-level

`TabRootObservationTests` asserts on the source text, which is unusual here and deliberate: the
cost is a property of **where the declaration sits**, and no runtime assertion on these views can
observe it. The next person to wire a tab-level dependency will not have read the comment on
`SleepView` either — that comment was already there, and it did not stop this.

## The symptom confirmed the diagnosis

Reported afterwards: the choppiness was **worst on Sleep and Trends**. That is exactly the ordering
this defect predicts — `SleepView` is 3046 lines, `TrendsView` 948, `DayQualityView` 432. The same
wrong subscription sat on all three; the cost scaled with the size of the body being re-evaluated.
A cause that produced uniform slowness across the three, or that hit Recap hardest, would have
meant the fix was aimed at the wrong thing.

## Verification

Both app targets build. 2430 tests, 2 failures — `SkinTempAbsoluteDisplayTests` and
`SleepCarriedStampTests`, both upstream's own files, both failing identically on a clean v11.8.0
checkout with no fork code present.
