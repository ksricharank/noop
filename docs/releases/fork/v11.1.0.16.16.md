# 11.1.0.16.16 (build 328)

The widget-lag investigation closes. The 260906-2052 log measured what WidgetKit does with our
requests, and the answer changes the fix from "ask more often" to "ask better".

## What the measurement said

    Widget background: publishes=235 reloads=66 dedup=169 unseen=0
    Widget timelines:  requested=108 served=224 reqToBuild=169s avg / 946s max over 43

`reqToBuild` is the finding, and it is the number this whole thread was missing. When the app asks
for a reload, WidgetKit does not ask the extension to build for **169 seconds on average and up to
946** (15.8 min). So a glanced widget trails by ~3 min at best and ~16 min at worst, even though the
app published the instant the data landed.

That deferral is the budget pushing back. **66 background reloads is at or over the ~40–70/day
ceiling**, and an app over its budget is not refused — it is throttled. Asking more often would have
made the lag worse.

**A correction:** the previous notes said 18 background reloads against a 40–70 budget, "roughly a
quarter of the allowance", and treated that as headroom. That figure came from an hour-old log —
every counter there is day-keyed, so it was a partial day read before the day had happened. The full
day is 66. The headroom never existed, and the answer to "why not use it" is that we were already
over.

## Why coalescing, not a shorter interval

The same log shows the strap syncing in tight bursts: **172 offloads that collapse into 58 genuinely
distinct moments** once anything inside two minutes counts as one (mean burst 3.0, max 9, median 9.8
min between bursts).

Every sync in a burst lands rows, every one genuinely moves a rendered value, and every one therefore
spent its own budget reload. The dedup cannot help here — those changes are all real, just superseded
seconds later by the next sync in the same burst.

## The gate

- **Coalesce bursts** — a 120 s floor, matching the burst structure actually measured.
- **Pre-empt for what the wearer acts on** — a logged cup (often the strap double-tap, which has no
  on-screen feedback at all, so the widget is the only confirmation), a score change, or steps past a
  visible threshold. Calories and effort drift continuously and are deliberately not urgent.
- **A hard cap of 48/day**, below the bottom of the OS range, to stay inside the promptly-served
  regime rather than the throttled one.
- **A pace backstop** that engages only when spend is running ahead of an even burn, so a busy
  morning cannot leave the evening stale.
- **Foreground reloads are not gated at all** — they are budget-exempt, so while the app is open the
  widget tracks it exactly.

A coalesced burst still **saves its snapshot**. The face shows the final state on the next build, and
the reload that does happen carries newer values than the first of nine would have. This is the rare
change where the cheaper option is also the fresher one.

The extension's own `.after` interval now adapts to the remaining budget — 10 minutes while budget
remains (shorter than the old flat 15, so self-builds fill the gaps between coalesced reloads),
stretching to 30 once spent. Those builds are not budget-charged, but each is a process wake.

## A design error the tests caught

The first cut made pacing the PRIMARY rate: remaining day ÷ remaining budget. At noon with a full
budget that is ~14 minutes — throttling a day that had spent nothing *harder than* a busy one, which
is backwards. A day has bursts and quiet stretches, and spending faster during a burst is correct.
Pacing is now a backstop on the run rate, and urgency is never paced.

Replaying the log against the final policy: **43 spent instead of 66 (−35 %), nothing capped, and a
reload in every hour through 20:00.**

A new `Widget budget:` line reports spend/cap, coalesced and capped, so the next log says whether the
pacing is working rather than leaving a silent skip.

## Also in this log: re-scoring is now the largest battery item

The 16.13 instrumentation produced its first real read, and it is stark:

    Re-score today: 73/76 done, 96 dropped — cpu bg=6946s fg=1706s · longest 4214s (forced)
    Re-score by trigger: light-today 57/58  post-offload 3/3  forced 13/15
    Re-score dropped: outstanding 77  locked 2  backstop-skip 17

**6946 s is 1.93 hours of background CPU on re-scoring alone**, against MetricKit's `bg=252m` — so
roughly 46 % of the day's background bill. One forced pass ran **4214 s (70 minutes)**. And 96
triggers were dropped, 77 of them because a pass was already outstanding: work asked for and thrown
away.

Not changed in this build — it is a scheduling problem of its own and deserves its own change rather
than riding along with the widget work. But it is now attributed, which is what 16.13 was for.

## What the next install must show

1. **`Widget budget:` in the log** — `spent=N/48 coalesced=N`, with `capped=0`. Spend well under 48
   with most bursts coalesced is the healthy shape.
2. **`reqToBuild` falling.** This is the real test: fewer requests should mean each is served
   promptly, so the average should drop well below 169 s. If it does not, the deferral is not
   budget-driven and the next lever is elsewhere.
3. **A logged cup still appears on the widget promptly** — water pre-empts the coalescer by design.
4. **Steps/calories on the face within a couple of minutes of the app**, not sixteen.

alarming — 27 s per sync), and the stress retro-scan reports `maxMs=4214336`, which is 70 minutes and
almost certainly the same forced re-score being timed inside it.
