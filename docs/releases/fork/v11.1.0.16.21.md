# 11.1.0.16.21 (build 333)

Build 332's day-quality score was wrong on screen, and the field cards said so precisely. Three
defects, one of them the formula itself. This build replaces the formula and fixes the two bugs that
were hiding how wrong it was.

Maintainer: *"something is off with how the scores are being computed… we should perhaps simplify the
formula."* Correct on both counts.

## What the cards showed

| day | headline | rows actually summed to |
|---|---|---|
| Sep 7 | +29 | 31 |
| Sep 3 | **+56** | **13** |
| Sep 2 | **+54** | **23** |

Three normal days reading 29–56, and two of them with a headline that had nothing to do with the
breakdown printed underneath it.

## Defect 1 — the formula: the offset dominated the achievement

Sep 7's steps row is the whole story: **9489 of 9550 steps — MISSING the target — scored +9.7 of
23.4.** Nearly half the available credit, for a miss.

16.20 derived the scale's origin from the scorer and then spread that origin across the components.
Each row therefore gained about **+8.7 points before any achievement was counted**, so the offset was
worth more than the performance and every ordinary day floated into the high twenties and fifties.
The `+0` anchor was real but only at one exact point; everywhere else the offset was just a pedestal.

The fix is the simplification asked for. The origin is now an **absolute, stated fact** about a
sedentary day rather than a derived quantity:

    normal day = 4000 steps · 1600 kcal · 8 effort · 8 cups · 7 h sleep

and each component is **one linear ramp from it: 0 at normal, its full weight at target**. At normal
input every term is *literally zero*, which makes the anchor STRUCTURAL rather than fitted — it cannot
drift when a weight changes, and there is nothing left to spread.

Absolute on purpose. Deriving the anchor from the wearer's own quietest days would reintroduce exactly
the relative drift the maintainer ruled out: a declining month would walk the zero point down with it
and a deconditioning stretch would keep reading zero. It is tunable instead, because "no deliberate
activity" is a fact about a person rather than a constant.

### Calibration

`targetPointsTotal` is 50 at the maintainer's choice — meeting your targets is halfway up the positive
half, leaving real room above it.

| day | 16.20 | **16.21** |
|---|---|---|
| **sedentary, normal activity, 7 h sleep** | ~58 | **0** |
| hit every target, signals at baseline | +58 | **+50** |
| beat every target, good recovery | +91 | +91 |
| everything maxed | +100 | **+100** |
| no exercise but 9 h sleep | +29 | +29 |
| little done, 5 h sleep | −23 | −48 |
| nothing done, poor recovery | −100 | −76 |

The three field days move from **+29 / +56 / +54** to **+23 / +20 / +28** — modestly positive days
that missed most of their targets, which is what they were.

Weights are relative and renormalised to `targetPointsTotal`, so tuning one in settings cannot break
the +50 anchor. `overshootCap` (1.7) is not taste: the five target-bearing components sum to 50 and
the two baseline signals add 15 at their ceiling, so 50 × 1.7 + 15 = 100 exactly.

## Defect 2 — the headline and the breakdown were two different computations

The headline read the **stored series**; the breakdown was **recomputed live**. Sep 3 showed +56 above
rows summing to 13 because the stored value came from the previous formula and nothing on screen could
reveal the disagreement.

Two sources for one number is the defect — whichever is stale, the card lies. The headline now prefers
the live breakdown, with the stored value kept only as a pre-load placeholder, so the two can never be
displayed side by side.

**The reconciliation test passed throughout.** It compared one `score()` result against itself and
never checked the card's two sources against each other — a test that verified the arithmetic while
the screen was free to show something else entirely.

## Defect 3 — the score was gated behind a pass that keeps failing

Day-quality scoring sat inside `if !lightPass`, and the comment justifying it said a 2-day window
could not compute the scored-night fields the recovery half needs. **That was false.** The function
takes no rows from the calling pass at all: it reads the full history from `repo.days` and carries its
own once-per-day latch. The `dailies` parameter it accepted was **never read**, and its presence is
what made the gate look justified.

Full passes are precisely the ones deferred and abandoned under the battery policy — `forced 15/49`
with repeated `gave up` in the 260908 log. So the score, *and the history re-derivation 16.20 promised
would run on first launch*, simply never ran. That is why Sep 2 and Sep 3 were still showing 0–100
values: the migration mechanism was sound and never got the chance to execute.

Now outside the gate, the dead parameter dropped, and a test asserts the call site's **indentation**
(8, not 12) so it cannot silently move back inside. An unusual test, but the call lives deep in a long
async function with no seam to inject, and the failure mode is invisible — the score just stops being
written.

Scale version bumped to **v3**, so every stored day re-derives. History values WILL change on
first launch; the old numbers were computed by a formula that has now been retired twice.

## Two knobs that stopped meaning anything

Handled rather than left to rot, because a control that moves nothing is worse than no control:

- **`overshootCap`** is rewired to the live overshoot multiple, so the existing slider still moves
  something real.
- **`executionShare`** is retired. The new scale has no execution/recovery split for it to divide —
  each component contributes its own absolute points, and the "halves" on the card are now a grouping
  for reading rather than a budget. It is kept only so a stored preference decodes, documented as
  inert, and a test pins that it genuinely is.

## Also fixed: the disappearing Steps row

Sep 2's card had **no Steps line at all** on a day with 8288 steps. Its target was 4000 — at or below
the normal-day reference, because a low-charge day shrinks targets — and the ramp had no span, so the
component was dropped entirely. A missing row reads as a data gap, not as an active day. It now scores
against the target itself (+15), because a target below the normal day is a statement about the
TARGET, not a reason to un-score the day.

## Verification

macOS and iOS both build. **1,817 StrandAnalytics tests and 1,849 app-target tests pass**, apart from
the known `SkinTempAbsoluteDisplay` timezone flake. Rows now sum to the headline in every case tested,
including both clamps and the target-met day whose raw sum is zero.

The Android twin is still NOT updated — Apple-only until the Kotlin scorer is moved the same way, and
the numbers will disagree across platforms until it is.

## What the next log must show

1. **Day quality on the new scale**: a sedentary day near **0**, not the high fifties. And the
   headline must equal the sum of the rows beneath it on every card — that is now a property of the
   code, so a disagreement means something else is wrong.
2. **All 47 days re-derived onto one scale.** The trend, the calendar strip and the week comparison
   must agree; any mixture of old and new values means the migration still is not running, and the
   next step is a diagnostic line from `scoreDayQuality` itself rather than another inferred fix.
3. **Re-score CPU back under ~900 s**, with `gave up` firing in seconds rather than hundreds of
   seconds (the 16.20 launch-seed fix, still unverified in the field).
4. Ignore `reqToBuild max` if it reads exactly **900 s** — that value is under suspicion as a clamp in
   our own instrumentation, not a measurement.
5. Carried forward, unchanged: the 54-hour light-pass read window remains the largest known battery
   cost (~59 % of every light pass's ~5 s of SQL, 187 times a day). It feeds the scoring path, so it
   needs an oracle proving byte-identical scores before it can be narrowed.
