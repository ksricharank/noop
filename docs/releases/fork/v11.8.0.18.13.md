# 11.8.0.18.13 (build 414)

The two sleep targets finally explained and resolved, and the metric widgets rebuilt from the
maintainer's verdict on 18.12.

## Why the two sleep numbers never matched

Asked twice, and the honest answer took three builds to find. They were **never the same
quantity**, and nothing on either screen said so:

| Screen | Showed | Which is |
|---|---|---|
| Sleep tab | **8h24m** | `ledger.needMin` — the **baseline**: the 75th percentile of this wearer's own recent nights. What a normal night looks like for them. |
| Today | **7h50** | `sleepNeedTonightMin` — **tonight's** target: the population floor, adjusted by charge, last night's Rest, the readiness read and debt payback. |

Both correct. The nap divergence fixed in 18.12 was a genuine bug — three ledgers crediting sleep
three different ways — but a **different** one, which is why fixing it did not make these two agree
and why the report came back.

Relabelling two surfaces and hoping the distinction lands would have been the fragile fix. The
target instead moves to the one screen that owns sleep, which was the maintainer's own suggestion
and the smaller change.

**Water takes the vacated fourth slot**, with its half-cup −/+ controls stacked under the value. A
grid cell is about half the width of the full-width row water had, and `17/21 cups` plus two 30pt
buttons does not fit on one line there — `minimumScaleFactor` would have shrunk the number to keep
them company, which is the opposite of what a targets strip is for.

The sleep plan also left Today's LLM context for the Sleep tab's facts, gated to the **current**
night so stepping back through history is not answered with advice about tonight.
`AICoachDailyTargetsBlockTests` asserted that line was present and now asserts it is absent, so a
future re-add has to be deliberate.

## The metric widgets, rebuilt

> I like the new rows and heatmap widgets the best … delete the overlay one - it is useless

Correct, and for a reason worth recording: normalising every metric to its own 0–1 range made the
lines comparable in **shape** and unreadable in **value**, so the legend underneath ended up doing
the real work. A chart whose legend carries the information is not a chart.

The two survivors are now **separate sections** rather than styles of one, so both sit on the page
at once. Each keeps:

- its own **metric list**,
- its own **order** — drag to reorder, swipe to hide, tap to add,
- its own **window selector** (1w / 2w / 1m / 3m), independent of the page's range bar. A heatmap
  reads best over weeks and a row stack over days; one shared control cannot serve both.

### Two rendering bugs, both caught from screenshots

**Rows overflowed.** Each strip's area wash bled down across the rows beneath it, so seven strips
rendered as one smear. `.frame(height:)` bounds a chart's **layout**, not its plot's **drawing** —
an `AreaMark` fills to the plot baseline and SwiftUI Charts will happily paint that fill outside the
frame it was given. `.chartPlotStyle { $0.clipped() }` is what confines it.

**The heatmap drew fifteen cells for a one-week window.** The shared axis unioned every metric's own
points *after* each had already been clipped to the window independently — so metrics recorded on
different days (steps on a day with no sleep) contributed different sets, and the union came out
longer than the window itself. Now it unions first and clips last, which also means a metric missing
a day renders a **gap** on the shared axis rather than shifting every later column left. Pinned by a
regression test built on two metrics with deliberately disjoint days.
