Two of the last build's three fixes worked decisively. The third was falsified by its own
measurement, and this build acts on that.

## The widget budget theory was wrong

16.16 predicted that spending fewer background reloads would buy faster service. The prediction was
explicit and testable. It failed:

    before (66 spent): reqToBuild=169s avg /  946s max
    after  (42 spent): reqToBuild=186s avg / 2044s max

**Spend fell 36 % and the deferral got worse.** WidgetKit was never throttling us for overspending —
the deferral is iOS scheduling on its own terms, and promptness cannot be bought with restraint. The
tight cap cost freshness and bought nothing.

So the cap is retired to a runaway guard (200; the coalescer already ceilings real traffic at ~62
reloads/day, so anything above ~70 never binds). The **pace backstop goes with it** — it existed
solely to ration a scarce allowance, and without one it would throttle a normal day for no benefit,
the same mistake one layer down. `nextTimelineInterval` is re-keyed on absolute spend, because
`dailyCap − usedToday` at cap 200 would have reported "plenty left" all day and never reached the
longer intervals: a threshold silently detuned by a change elsewhere.

**The coalescer is now the only rate limit**, which is what the evidence supports. Five tests
encoded the dead theory and are INVERTED rather than deleted — the wrong version is exactly what a
future reader would otherwise re-derive from the 16.16 notes.

## Day quality: HRV was not missing, it was miscalibrated

Reported as HRV/HR data missing from the score. It was not: the card read `HRV 30 ms vs 36 ms
baseline → 1.7/15`, and the arithmetic reproduces that exactly. Nothing was broken in the plumbing —
but 1.7 out of 15 reads as a collection failure rather than as a slightly-off night, which is why it
looked like one.

One downside slope (zero at 20 % below baseline) was applied to every signal. Pooling 23 banked
nights, this wearer's night-to-night HRV deviation from its own median is **7 % (median), 21 % (p75),
34 % (p90)** — so a slope zeroing at −20 % put a ROUTINE night at the bottom of the scale. HRV is
simply that volatile; that is a fact about the signal, not about the day.

HRV widens to **−40 %** (zero near p90 of the wearer's own spread). That night now scores **6.6/15**.

Resting HR deliberately **keeps −20 %**: it varies far less night to night — the same nights that
swung HRV 34 % moved RHR by a handful of bpm — so widening it too would flatten a signal that
carries real information when it does move. One constant for both was the actual bug; the fix is
per-signal, not merely bigger.

History re-scores on the next config change, the mechanism that already exists for a formula move.

## Water tap: a confirmation with Undo, and a configurable window

The tap already buzzed, but a buzz says only "something happened" — on a gesture with no on-screen
feedback that is the whole question. The notification names the cup and the running total, because
the total is what makes an accidental log obvious.

**Undo is the load-bearing half.** The complaint is about false positives, and a confirmation alone
would only make them visible — the wearer would still open the app to correct one. It carries the
ENTRY ID captured at post time, not an amount: undoing "the most recent cup" would delete a
reminder-driven cup or an in-app +Cup that landed in between.

The gesture window is configurable at the top of the Automations double-tap card, defaulting to
**2 s** — the old hard-coded 1.2 s is the value the complaint was raised against, so shipping it as
the default would have been a setting that changes nothing.

Worth stating what this knob is NOT: one physical tap firing exactly once stays structural (the
16.13 event-timestamp dedup) and must never become a setting. This is the separate question of how
far apart two SEPARATE taps must be.

## Weekly summary on both per-metric tabs, and a better Day icon

The Trends digest could not be reused — it is built from `WeeklyMetric` over `DailyMetric` rows, and
day quality has no such column. Its SHAPE ported over: browsable weeks with chevrons, the
days-with-data count, week-over-week, best/worst. Monday-anchored to match `WeeklyDigestEngine`, so
the two screens cannot disagree about which days are in a week.

The Day tab icon becomes `medal` — a grade, which is what the tab shows. Not `sparkles`: that is
already the Coach's mark in two places.
