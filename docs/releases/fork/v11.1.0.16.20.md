# 11.1.0.16.20 (build 332)

The day-quality score gets the scale it should always have had, the Day tab gets the depth it was
missing, and the re-score abort gate that looked correct in one log turns out to have been armed only
for the passes that did not need it.

## What the 260908 log settled

| | 09-07 | 09-08 |
|---|---|---|
| **Widget budget spent** | 42/48 (capped) | **71/200** (never binds) |
| widget coalesced | 32 | **13** |
| `reqToBuild` avg | 186 s | 283 s |
| **Re-score background CPU** | 869 s | **1531 s** |
| longest pass | 33 s | **401 s** |
| gave up (backgrounded mid-pass) | ×16 | **×28** |
| **Health sync per pass** | 5.1 s | **0.6 s** |
| health-sync reads | 4 s | **343 ms** |

Removing the widget cap worked mechanically: 71 background reloads, nothing capped, coalescing
pressure down from 32 to 13. Health sync improved again, 8.5× on the read phase.

**Two claims from the last build are corrected here.** First, `reqToBuild` avg got *worse* (186 →
283 s) while spend rose 69 % — a third independent measurement against "spending buys freshness",
and the theory stays dead. Second, the apparent tail improvement (2044 → 900 s max) should not be
read as a win: **900 s is exactly 15 minutes**, which is far more likely a ceiling in our own
instrumentation than an observed value. Reported as a fix last time; withdrawn as unattributable now.

## The abort gate was blind on a cold background launch

Re-score regressed hard — CPU +76 %, one pass logging `gave up after 1021 s`. The 16.17 abort gate
should have stopped that at the first day boundary. The log says why it did not:

    [17:53:06] re-score: trigger=forced where=background newData=yes
    ...
    [18:10:07] re-score: gave up after 1021 s of a full pass

That pass **began** in the background, during a restored-peripheral relaunch. The gate reads a
nonisolated mirror of the scene phase, written by `.onChange(of: scenePhase)` — and `.onChange` fires
on a TRANSITION. A cold launch straight into the background never transitions, so nothing had ever
written the mirror and it still held its initial `false`: *"we are in the foreground."*

So the gate was enabled for passes that started in front of the user and then left — the case it was
written for, which is exactly why it looked correct in the 260906 log — and **disabled for the ones
that were in the background the whole time**, which are the expensive ones.

The fix distinguishes a MEASUREMENT from a DEFAULT: the mirror now records whether a real transition
has been seen, and until one has, the gate falls back to what the app reported about itself at launch
(published once from `StrandiOSApp.init`, where `UIApplication.applicationState` is legitimately
reachable — it is main-actor-only, which is the whole reason the detached gate needs a mirror). A
transition then takes precedence permanently; the seed is a pre-transition default, not a retroactive
override.

### A fix written, then reverted

I first added a mid-day abort inside `timedRead`, on the theory that day 0 was uninterruptible.
Walking it back against the log showed it was a **no-op for the logged case** — day 0 already
completes before the existing `offset > 0` check, so observable behaviour would have been identical.
Reverted rather than shipped. A plausible fix the log does not actually support is what the
instrumentation-first rule exists to prevent, and it is recorded in the commit so it is not
re-derived later.

### What the log points at next, and why it is not in this build

The dominant recurring cost is now attributable with no guesswork:

    analyzeRecent cost prep=5128ms (read=5127ms compute=0ms) score=487ms
    analyzeRecent store sql reads=5107ms/19 writes=0ms
    analyzeRecent dayCache reused=1/2 size=2 days=2

`compute=0ms` — the math is free; reads are ~90 % of every pass. `sql reads ≈ read` — the time is real
SQL execution, not actor queueing. And `days=2` on 17 of 18 passes means these are LIGHT passes
(`light-today 187/187`), each spending ~5 s of SQL to score two days, one of which it already has
cached. **187 × ~5 s ≈ 16 minutes of SQL per day**, materialising 2,389,825 HR rows.

The cause is the 54-hour read window (`dayStart - 30h`), of which ~59 % is pre-midnight lookback a
light pass cannot use — its stated job is "advance TODAY's accumulators". And the log contains its own
proof that removing it works: two passes hit the day cache fully and cost `read=0ms` instead of
~5000 ms.

It is NOT in this build. That window feeds the sleep stager, respiration, gravity and step reads on
the **scoring** path, where an optimisation may only ever skip work and never change a number.
Narrowing it needs an oracle proving the scores are byte-identical first. That is its own change with
its own verification, not a rider on this one.

Also explained and dismissed: `Stress retro-scan maxMs=401327` looked alarming, but
`maxWait=401324ms` against `maxReplay=59ms` says the scan was **queued behind the re-score**, not
working — and 401 s is precisely the re-score's `longest 401s`. One stall counted twice, `blocked=1`
confirming a single spike. Fixing the read cost fixes this for free.

## Day quality is now a signed −100…+100 score

The 0–100 scale could not express what the score is for. Its bottom band called an ordinary sedentary
day "Light" at ~58, it had no negative half at all, and its own arithmetic overflowed the top: a
merely *good* day already summed to 103 and was clamped, so real range was being thrown away — the
ceiling problem the HRV recalibration note warned about, applied to the total.

Maintainer's specification: a sedentary day with no exercise and normal sleep is **0**, a good day
rises to **+100**, a poor day falls to **−100**.

Each component is now scored as **signed credit** against a neutral point rather than as a fraction of
a maximum. Hitting a target contributes 0; beating it earns credit up to the overshoot cap; missing it
costs credit down to zero output. The ratio components are neutral at target, and HRV/resting HR are
neutral at the 0.75 that `baselineAchievement` already places "at baseline" — so the autonomic signals
needed no new calibration.

| day | score |
|---|---|
| **sedentary, no exercise, 7 h sleep** | **0** |
| hit every target, signals at baseline | +58 |
| 2026-09-08 (this log's own day) | +26 |
| good day (+15 % on targets) | +74 |
| **every target beaten** | **+100** |
| rest day: no exercise, great sleep | +29 |
| sedentary + 5 h sleep | −23 |
| poor day | −77 |
| **nothing done, poor recovery** | **−100** |

A fixed bias term was the maintainer's suggestion and is the right instinct, but a bias **alone**
cannot do it: one that puts the sedentary day at 0 leaves the worst possible day at −63, so −100
becomes unreachable. It takes two slopes through three anchors — the `stepsBaseForCharge` technique.
The anchors are **derived from the scorer**, not written down, so changing a weight or a tolerance
moves them automatically and the published scale keeps meaning what it says.

The zero anchor is deliberately not knife-edged: it holds within **±2 points** across sleep needs from
7 h to 9 h, and **±6** across a plausible span of "no deliberate activity". That robustness is what
makes a single fixed anchor honest rather than a fitted constant.

### Two regressions the rescale introduced, both caught by the existing tests

Worth naming, because inspection would not have found either:

- **The load factor went completely INERT.** Multiplying credit was correct on a scale where meeting a
  target scored 1.0; on the signed scale it scores exactly **0**, and zero times anything is zero. Two
  days that both hit 100 % of very different targets published the same number while correctly
  reporting load factors of 1.075 and 0.925. Silently dead is worse than absent — the factor still
  appeared in the breakdown as though it had done something. It is now an additive tilt, still clamped
  to ±15 % and halved at default strength.
- **The anti-gaming bound disappeared.** Normalising the upside to +1 made a 40 000-step day with no
  workout score *identically* to a day that hit every target: +1 and −1 cancelled exactly. Both
  directions are now measured in shared units — fractions of the neutral value — so the full overshoot
  allowance is worth 0.25 against a miss's 1.0. That 4:1 asymmetry **is** the bound, and it came from
  the old scale's own 0.25-vs-1.0 spread rather than from a new rule.

The origin shift is folded into each component *before* summing, which keeps the published scale a
pure multiplier and the breakdown panel reconciling with the headline — at both clamps and on a
target-met day whose raw sum is exactly zero. Adding the offset to the SUM instead left the rows
adding to a number the screen never shows, and zeroed every row on that day. Three attempts; a test
now pins all six cases.

### History is re-derived, not mixed

All 47 stored days re-derive onto the signed scale on first launch. Rather than a bespoke migration,
the scale version rides in `configFingerprint` and reuses the path a moved slider already takes —
`configChanged` goes true once and `rescoreAll` clears the "already scored" set. Idempotent, because a
finished day's INPUTS are immutable: recomputation applies the current formula to the same rows, and a
rollback re-derives back.

Two hardcoded `min(upperBound, 100)` clamps in `ScoreTrendSection` are gone. They would have folded
the entire negative half onto the chart's floor — a bug that looks like missing data rather than a
scaling error.

## The Day tab, and the rest of the ask

- **Bar chart, not a curve.** `TrendChart` already had a bar mode whose baseline logic literally
  anticipated this case (`min(0, lowerBound)`, with a comment about a caller who might one day plot
  negatives), so this is a flag rather than a new chart. A signed score reads better as bars: the zero
  line becomes visible geometry, and each day is a discrete verdict where a curve implies a continuous
  quantity interpolating between days it never measured.
- **Three Insights cards** below the coach narrative — cards, not notifications, as asked. *What moved
  the score* ranks every component by weighted contribution and names the biggest drag outright.
  *Closest gains* prices what closing each unmet target is worth, in the same points the headline
  shows; HRV and resting HR are excluded because they are scored against a baseline rather than a
  target you can decide to hit, so "N more ms of HRV" is not an action. *Consistency* counts days above
  zero — a statistic that only became meaningful once zero was the sedentary anchor — and says
  explicitly that an unscored day neither extends nor breaks a run.
- **The tab is arrangeable.** Nine cards through `DaySection` order + visibility, twin of
  `SleepLayoutPrefs`; the date navigator and score card stay pinned as the frame. The coach narrative
  was in the enum and is now deliberately out: it lives inside the score card's collapsible area, so a
  "Narrative" row would have done nothing when hidden — a control that lies is worse than no control.
- **Trends carries both composite scores.** Day quality joins Charge · Effort · Rest in Week in
  review (the only signed row, so it passes its own range and derives its vessel fill from that
  range — a negative average would otherwise render identically to a zero one). Both scores appear as
  small multiples in Daily signals, as week means under the digest, and as full-year calendar strips.
  `WeeklyMetric` is left **alone**: it is a shared analytics enum with a byte-identical Kotlin twin,
  and day quality does not exist on Android yet, so adding a case would either break parity or block
  this on porting the feature. The week's mean is computed in the view instead, and a test pins the
  enum's shape.
- **Trends moves to the fourth tab.** Order is now Today · Day · Sleep · Trends · More. The reorder is
  one line; the reason it touched more is that every tab index was a raw literal, and the Trends
  deep-link was `selectedTab = 1` — which after the move opens Day quality: a tab that loads perfectly
  and is simply the wrong one, with nothing to indicate it. Indices now live in `PhoneTab`, in a file
  both platforms compile, so the macOS test leg can pin an order that `RootTabView` (iOS-only, no CI)
  previously made untestable.
