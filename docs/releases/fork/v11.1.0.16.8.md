# 11.1.0.16.8 (build 320)

Instrumentation only: measure why the widgets lag behind the app. **No behaviour change** — every
publish, dedup and reload decision is byte-identical to 319.

Build 319 confirmed on the strap: the steps toggle works ("the steps count is a faithful map of the
apple health steps") and the water double-tap updates immediately. Both of 16.6/16.7's open items
are now closed.

## The report, and why the first answer was wrong

"The widgets don't seem to update when the app does but is not in the foreground."

My first answer was that the water path was covered, because its publish hook is deliberately NOT
scenePhase-gated. That was reading the hook and calling it evidence; the maintainer corrected it —
water lags too. Reading the code proves what we ASK for, not what happens.

The evidence says our side is working. From the 260904 log:

    Widget publish today: full=162/163 live=27 reloads=53 dedup=111

162 publishes ran, 53 requested a WidgetKit reload, 111 correctly deduped as unchanged. Water IS
compared by `renderedContentChanged`, the snapshot IS written, `reloadAllTimelines()` IS called. The
request is made; something downstream decides when — or whether — it lands.

## What this build adds, and why measurement rather than a fix

The instrumentation-first rule applies exactly here: the existing line raises the question and cannot
answer it, for two independent reasons.

**1. `reloads` is a total, and only half of it is charged.** Background-initiated WidgetKit reloads
count against an OS budget (~40-70/day, the estimate already recorded in `renderedContentChanged`);
foreground ones are exempt. A day of 53 foreground reloads is healthy; a day of 53 background ones
is starving. The total cannot tell them apart. Now split, with the first and last background reload
of the day — a cluster that stops mid-morning is the deferral signature, and is exactly what "the
widget lags behind the app" looks like from the inside.

**2. Nothing measured what WidgetKit actually SERVED.** Every counter on the publish path describes
what the APP requested. The widget extension now records each `getTimeline` call into the shared App
Group, so the log carries requested vs served. That separates three diagnoses a single number
cannot:

  * served ≈ requested → the pipeline delivers; a stale face means the snapshot was stale when read,
    so look upstream at the publish inputs.
  * served ≪ requested → iOS is dropping or deferring requests: the budget story, fixed by spending
    fewer background reloads.
  * served ≈ the 15-minute policy's own count (~96/day) while requested is high → our reload
    requests are not landing at all, which is a different bug from budget exhaustion.

Two integers and a timestamp, written where `getTimeline` already runs. The extension is
memory-constrained and killed aggressively, so it allocates nothing and blocks on nothing.

## A real finding, deliberately left unfixed

The new `unseen` counter tracks background reloads whose only changed field no installed widget face
renders — budget spent on a repaint nobody can see.

That is not hypothetical. `sleepDisplay` is still compared by the dedup but was dropped from the
faces when water took Sleep's fourth cell (260903); the comparison stayed behind. So a night's sleep
figure moving still requests a reload, and in the background that comes straight out of the
allowance the visible updates need.

**Measured, not removed**, for two reasons. The count decides whether it is worth changing at all.
And the obvious fix is wrong in one real case: a snapshot whose only change is sleep must still be
SAVED — it feeds the glance string this very log prints — just not repainted. That is a different
change from deleting the comparison, and worth making only once the number justifies it.

The probe neutralises the known-unrendered field and re-asks `renderedContentChanged`, rather than
restating the visible-field list. So a field added to the dedup counts as VISIBLE until someone
explicitly names it here, and the two lists cannot drift apart.

## What the next log must show

This build exists to produce one line-triple, so read it before changing anything:

```
Widget publish today: full=… live=… reloads=… dedup=… last=… steps=… water=…
Widget background:    publishes=… reloads=… dedup=… unseen=… window=HH:MM-HH:MM
Widget timelines:     requested=… served=… last=HH:MM
```

1. **`Widget background: reloads=`** against the ~40-70 budget. Near or above it, with a `window`
   that stops early, is budget exhaustion — and then reducing background spend is the fix.
2. **`served` vs `requested`.** A large shortfall confirms iOS is dropping requests. `served=0`
   with a live widget on screen is itself a finding: the extension is not being launched at all.
3. **`unseen`.** A material count justifies dropping the `sleepDisplay` comparison from the reload
   decision (while keeping the save). A near-zero count means that idea is not worth the change.
4. **`Widget background: none today`** would be the surprise: it would mean the scenePhase gating,
   not the budget, is what keeps the widget stale — a different fix entirely.

A log taken a day or two after install is worth far more than one taken an hour after, since every
counter here is day-keyed and the budget story only appears over a full day.
