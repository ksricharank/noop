## 1. A configurable NSS alert level (default 50)

"I want a configurable parameter for NSS at which point I get a notification for high stress. make the
default as 50. Add this config to the stress tab (at the bottom)."

- `NSSNotifier.threshold` is stored under `nss.notifyThreshold`: 10–95, default **50** (it was a fixed
  67). It re-arms once NSS falls **7 below** the level.
- The other gates are unchanged: fresh readings only, once per stretch, at most hourly.
- A new **Alerts** card is the last section of the Stress tab. It holds the on/off switch (moved off the
  method card) and "Alert at NSS [50] Apply", which shows the range and my typical.
- The chart key now reads "You're alerted at N". The log line names the level:
  `nss: high notification posted (NSS N ≥ alert L, M min old)`.

## 2. NSS charts: a flexible y-axis

"so that the space between 100 and the max NSS is not wasted empty space."

- **The top:** `NSSChartScale.top(peak:)` takes the day's highest reading or typical, adds 5, rounds up
  to a tick, and keeps the result between 30 and 100.
- **The ticks** are round: every 10 up to 40, every 20 up to 80, every 25 above.
- **One function** for the app charts and the widget (`StrandiOSShared/StressTrace.swift`, compiled into
  both apps and the widget).
- **Colours stay true:** `DaytimeLoadLine` takes a `domainMax` and samples its colour ramp over the
  visible range. A calm day zoomed to 0–40 stays blue and green rather than turning amber at the top of
  the box. The widget's stroke does the same with its band tint.
- Rendered: a day peaking at 45 draws on 0 · 20 · 40 · 60.

## 3. Water target: 19 cups was too high

"default of 19 cups seems high. should this be like 11 or 12?" Yes.

- **Why it was 19:** the cups target started from `baselineForSex`, 3.7 L for men (16 cups), plus one
  cup per 10 points of effort target (+3 that day). But 3.7 L is the US National Academies' adequate
  intake of TOTAL water, and about a fifth of that comes from food. The Academies' figure for
  **beverages** is about 3.0 L (men) and 2.2 L (women). That counts coffee, tea and milk, which the
  tracker's taps do not. EFSA's figures are lower again, about 2.0 L and 1.6 L from drinks.
- **The fix:** `HydrationGoal.drinkingBaselineCups` sets **12 cups (2.8 L) for men**, 9 for women and 11
  when unspecified. The effort bump is unchanged.
- That day reads **15 cups** instead of 19; a rest day reads 12. The explainer says "12 cups to drink".
  The ml goal and its Android twin are untouched (the cups path is fork-only).

## 4. Recap: the calorie row was broken, not just harsh

"I got -13.9 out of 11 for accruing 1321 of 1840 cals which seems harsh."

- **The cause:** since 19.28 a day's calories are its whole burn (resting all day plus exercise), and the
  target is resting burn plus the session. The scorer still used a fixed normal-day zero of 1600, which
  sits BELOW a full day of pure rest (about 1700 for the maintainer) and only 240 kcal under the 1840
  target.
- **The effect:** one point per 22 kcal. The day credited 1321 ran (1321 − 1600)/240 × 11 = −12.8, and
  the load factor took it to −13.9.
- **Why 1321 at all:** a whole day cannot burn less than its resting burn, so 1321 means the day was
  not fully recorded. Either the strap was off, or the stored total ends at the day's last reading.
- **The fix:**
  - The ramp's zero is the wearer's resting burn: `DayQualityScore.normalKcal` = `kcalResting`, which is
    BMR × 24 h, the target's own floor.
  - The target is the full weight, and the row **never goes below zero**.
  - A total under resting burn shows "… under your resting 1700, day not fully recorded".
  - The "Normal calories" knob is retired (the settings caption reads "your resting burn"), and the
    Closest-gains card uses the same zero.
  - That day now scores **0** on calories instead of −13.9.
- The other rows were checked and left alone. Steps (4000 → target), effort (8 → target), water (8 cups
  → target) and sleep (7 h → need) all ramp from a real "nothing deliberate" anchor, and their negatives
  mean what they say.
